I was in the middle of something, something vast that I'd lost track of. I believe I was in the middle of something. Nothing grand, but rather some mundane activity that any typical layman would find themselves doing. It was some repetitive work, and I remember thinking to myself: what a waste of time! Then an instance later, I forgot where I was. I still remembered that I'd been somewhere and was in the middle of some terrible boring work. Other than those two pieces of information, I had no other memory. My mind felt empty. Yes, I remembered how the world worked and how the planet had four seasons and all the natural wonders of our globe. I knew the political science I was taught in university, a sufficient deal of mathematics, and the typical know-how of a standard education in a globalized and institutionalized civilization. I knew normal knowledge, but I'd forgotten everything about myself. 

What was my name? I didn't know. Where was I born? I didn't know. Did I have a family? No image of a mother flashed before eyes. I felt my mind had gone through some abrupt cleansing that made me forgot about myself. It was a self-dissociation. I had forgotten anything to do with the life I might have led, all in a personal sense. The feelings I felt were somewhat liberating.

I looked around myself. I was in an office building. I was sitting on a desk a paper leaflet on my hands. The building was uninhabited, but the strangest thing was that it seemed people were there just a moment ago. There was a half-finished lunch box on a nearby table. A steaming mug of coffee was placed on an adjunct desk. I looked at the other desks and saw personal belongings. Photos of smiling spouses, kids' birthday cards, custom-made stationary. I looked at my own desk. There was nothing personal about it. There was only work.

I got up from the chair. I didn't feel anything sinister, and everything seemed to be working fine. I could hear the dull hum of the central air conditioning unit. The floor lights were bright. I heard water gushing forth in a pipe overheard; must've been the building's water management system. There was nothing unusual. Everything that didn't have a human factor was still there. 

For the first few minutes, the thing that most unnerved me was how there were no traffic noises. Though I have no personal memories, I realized at the moment that whoever I was before this was someone who'd lived in this city for a long time, long enough to be disturbed when there were no traffic noises. I went near one of the windows and looked down below. No pedestrians. I could espy a stray cat roaming from corner to corner. Though there were cars in the roads none of them seemed to be moving. I was on a lower floor, so my view of things on the street was detailed. From here, there were no passengers in any of the cars as far as I could see. I looked at the other buildings and stared for a lengthy amount of time. No human face appeared in a window. No human figure either. There were no humans in this city that gave every hint of having them. There was trash and items you'd expect your normal pedestrian to drop off on the road without a tang of care. Of course anyone would assume this city was populated.

I traced the stairs on the floor. From there on, I walked down to the first floor. The first floor was the reception hall. You might think I'd have to have a queer memory to remember that but no personal details of my life. I do have a queer memory, but that isn't the reason. There was a big sign that said 'RECEPTION HALL' right over the reception desk. Clearly, I'd have to be an idiot to not infer that.

The reception hall seemed clean. Roughly clean. There was a half-eaten sandwich wrapped in its clear plastic packaging on the floor. Judging from how it was on the floor, it seemed someone had dropped it. Where had that someone gone? Did they just disappear? I let the sandwich be, and went out through the front door. The stray cat was a few blocks away. How dangerous were the city pets, now that there were no other humans? I looked around and went off to explore one of the parked cars.

It was a slick black SUV, a big bull among the other cars. It was the kind of vehicle I'd buy if I had the means to. The rims and fittings were silver, and was well kept. No major scratches nor bumps on its surface. I tried for the handle, but it was locked. Peering through the window, I saw there was nothing inside, yet it'd been locked as if it had passengers a mere moment ago.

Did I want to go inside so bad that I'd smash one of the windows with whatever sharp object I could find? I reckoned not because I wasn't that obsessed with the car. It was only a simple liking, a short infatuation with something interesting. I left the black SUV and drifted along. I didn't know what I was looking for. Maybe to see if there were any other people? Maybe I was just hungry and wanted a bite? Maybe I was a cat person and was unconsciously following that stray cat I'd seen before? No one needs a reason to travel. The purpose behind the journey is the journey itself, not the riches you acquire or the friendships you establish. Nothing arbitrary. It's only about the damn journey and nothing else, so I figured I didn't need to have a reason to be exploring. I don't even think I was in the search of something.

The absence of common city noises still felt profoundly new to my ears. It felt like I was wearing a heavy second set of skin, something invisible and limiting my every move. I could hear the wind breeze pass me, the branches of a tree bellow as they held the weight of a squirrel, a rat scurrying on the concrete floor. Were these the new noises the city would make? Was it just so sick of listening to humans that it made them disappear and invited new residents to dwell in its handsome quarters?
