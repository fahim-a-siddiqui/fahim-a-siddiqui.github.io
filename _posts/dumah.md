I should instead question how I can get out. Yes, isn't that better? To not dwell on the past's mistakes but instead partake in a perhaps successful future.

I had a gut feeling telling me it wasn't.

Evening was approaching, and I'd sat for the whole time. The sunlight was nearly out. Well, at least there was a sun and the evening looked normal. Everything that didn't have to deal with humans was familiar. I sat for longer and closed my eyes, thinking sleep would overtake me. Then suddenly I had a revelation: I didn't feel hungry at all! Even though I hadn't had a bite for hours, my stomach didn't feel empty. No, it was more like I didn't feel a stomach inside me which was probably more bizarre than whatever world I was supposed to be.

By the time I convinced myself that I did really have a stomach and that I might've just had a really big lunch before I came here, the sun was all out and it was night. The villagers had flamed their candles, and for a terrible second I felt a great longing for technology. It was very dark, darker than anything I'd been used to. Darker than the room I used to have at my parents' apartment when all the lights were out and it was well pass midnight. It was an insidious darkness that chewed on the borders of my vision, and for the first time ever since I'd been here, I realized I may have actually been trapped inside some fairytale land. And it wasn't even a unique fantasy world. Just populated with monsters and my atheist self reasoned what were men with special circumstances. There is no holiness in a world devoid of God.

As the night wore on, I debated whether I should try asking the villagers for lodging. I had no money, no belongings to speak of. Only clothes on me that I couldn't remove. No, it'd be a ridiculous thing to ask of. Free lodging? Yeah right, with all the soaring rent prices back home and here am I looking for free lodging. Humans are too cruel to be altruistic. All of them are. The nice ones are just looking to be rewarded in the future, be that with the improvement of reputation or the promise of a pleasant hereafter.

All those stars in the sky. If there'd been the light pollution I was used to, I wouldn't have to deal with such darkness but I also wouldn't have been able to have such a nice view of the night sky. I kept my eyes on the night sky, my gaze grazing over all those bright lights, all those distant worlds. Maybe I was part of some strange science experiment that transported me into this world, and my old home was out there somewhere orbiting those pale dots. At least that'd mean I wasn't in some rugged stereotypical fantasy world. Now that'd be just awful.

I heard something move in the darkness, something other than croaking crickets or rustling leaves. It was difficult to see in such little light, but I could see the frame of a woman.

"We're all monsters in this inhumane world, each capable of treachery or sainthood."

The woman was silent for a moment.

"Satisfactory answer. I didn't think I'd meet one of my kin here. Us monsters don't trek the north much. Something about a curse, I guess. Beats me." the woman said coming closer to me.

"You've some funny clothes on. Mind explaining?"

"Your clothes. They're stuck on you. Want me to try your pants?"

"You're taking this human thing too far. You don't have to fake it with me. I know you aren't one."

I sighed.

"You're not. I'm not a strong monster by any chance, but any monster can tell a human and something that isn't human from smell. And as I said before: you don't smell human."

"Anyways, I'm curious if your pants are stuck to you as well."

Before I could add a voice of protest, the woman rushed to me, closing the distance between us in a second, and grabbed my waist. Then she pulled down as hard as she could, but my clothes wouldn't bulge.

"Now, that's something--"

"Get off me you dirty cockroach!" I shouted, pushing her away.

I had to touch another human being. This was worse than being trapped in another world. The woman looked flustered. As she was much closer to me now, I could see her features far more defined than before even under the little light my eyes had; I'd become adjusted somewhat to the depravity of light. She smiled, revealing rows of unclean teeth.

Alright, this wasn't my world at all. I had to find a way to get out of here quick. There's certainly no way I can live my life without the internet and among peasants who all call me a freak and a humanoid brand of animals that can't bleed. No thanks, mister. Likely, there wasn't any phone nearby. If there was a phone, maybe I could call God and ask for a lift.

"Thanks for the demonstration. Could you leave now?"

The woman frowned.

"Don't tell me you're not hungry. I've been hungry for months, but the food here is difficult to catch. I'm Nora, by the way. Nice to, um, meet you?"

"I don't feel hunger. I am, um, Dumah. I may or may not talk much."

I've been managing by biting people when they sleep and draining enough of their blood that they don't feel anything's wrong and wake from their slumber. This diet has made me feel very weak. Anyways, with the two of us we'll be more capable than before."
